Student visas by country

How much money you must show, how many hours you may work while studying, and what you can do after graduating — from each government's official pages. Fields left out were not stated in the official sources. Rules change often: check the source links before applying.

Germany · United Kingdom · United States · Canada · Australia · Italy · Japan · South Korea · Malaysia · France · Netherlands · Ireland · Türkiye · Hungary

Germany

VisaNational Visa (Study)
Funds to showAt least €11,904 a year in a blocked account (€992 a month can be withdrawn) — or a scholarship, bank guarantee or formal obligation letter
Work while studying140 full or 280 half days a year without Federal Employment Agency approval; student-assistant jobs at the university are extra
After graduationNon-EU graduates of German universities get a job-seeker residence permit of up to 18 months (not renewable); any job allowed meanwhile, living costs must be covered; switch to a work permit (e.g. EU Blue Card) once employed

United Kingdom

VisaStudent visa
Funds to show£1,483 a month in London or £1,136 outside London (up to 9 months) + remaining first-year tuition; held for 28 consecutive days
Work while studying20 hours a week in term for full-time degree-level courses; full time in vacations
After graduationGraduate visa: 2 years if applying by 31 Dec 2026, 18 months from 1 Jan 2027; 3 years after a PhD
Visa fee£558 when applying from outside the UK (check gov.uk for the current fee)

United States

VisaF-1 student visa
Funds to showNo fixed national amount — show the total cost stated on your I-20
Work while studyingOn campus, up to 20 hours a week while classes are in session
After graduationOPT: up to 12 months per higher degree level; +24 months STEM OPT for degrees on the DHS STEM list with an E-Verify employer; unemployment limit 90 days (150 with STEM)
Visa feeVisa (MRV) fee $185 + SEVIS I-901 fee $350

Canada

VisaStudy permit
Funds to showSingle applicant: CAD 22,895 for living costs (from 1 Sep 2025) + first-year tuition + travel
Work while studyingOff campus up to 24 hours a week in term; unlimited in scheduled breaks
After graduationPGWP: up to 3 years after a programme of 2+ years; programme length for 8 months–2 years; 3 years after a master's of at least 8 months; PGWP-eligible institution, apply within 180 days, once in a lifetime

Australia

VisaStudent visa (subclass 500)
Funds to showAUD 29,710 for 12 months' living costs + first-year course fees + travel; or annual income of at least AUD 60,000
Work while studying48 hours per fortnight while the course is in session; unlimited in breaks; no limit for research master's and PhD students once the course starts
After graduationTemporary Graduate visa (subclass 485): 18 months to 3 years depending on the qualification

Italy

VisaStudy visa (type D)
Funds to show€10,179.85 a year (about €848 a month); some consulates also ask for return-travel funds (about €500)
Work while studyingUp to 20 hours a week and 1,040 hours a year; more during breaks if the annual cap is respected
After graduationJob-search permit (ricerca di lavoro) of 9–12 months without the 20-hour limit; convert to a work permit once employed

Japan

VisaStudent (College Student)
Work while studyingWith permission, 28 hours a week (8 hours a day in long vacations); permission can be obtained at the airport
After graduationIf no job yet: Designated Activities status for job hunting, 6 months, usually renewable once (a second year under conditions); school recommendation letter needed

South Korea

VisaD-2 student visa
Funds to showAt least KRW 900,000 a month × 6 months ≈ KRW 5,400,000
Work while studyingWith permission, about 30 hours a week (UG) or 35 (PG) — sources differ; Korean proficiency usually required
After graduationD-10-1 job-seeker visa for professional jobs (E-1–E-7 types); internships allowed; no financial proof on a first change from D-2

VisaStudent Pass
Work while studying20 hours a week at approved places — EMGS says only in breaks of more than 7 days, Immigration says also during studies; ask the institution
Visa feeStudent Pass RM60 a year + visa fee (by nationality, up to RM50) + EMGS processing fee

France

VisaLong-stay student visa (VLS-TS)
Funds to showAt least €877.50 a month from 1 Aug 2026 (previously €615), excluding tuition, for the whole stay
Work while studying964 hours a year (about 20 hours a week), counted annually
After graduationAfter a master's or equivalent: 12-month APS / job-search permit (usually not renewable); which one applies depends on nationality — ask the prefecture
Visa feeVisa fee €50 (per Campus France USA; may differ by country) + Campus France and VFS/TLS fees

Netherlands

VisaMVV + study residence permit
Funds to showIND study norm for university/hbo: €1,130.77 a month in 2026 (changes every 1 January); usually 12 months deposited with the university
Work while studyingUp to 16 hours a week, or full time only in June–August (not both in one year); employer needs a TWV work permit from UWV
After graduationOrientation year: 1 year to look for work within 3 years of graduating in the Netherlands, no work permit needed; not renewable; fee €254 (2026)

Ireland

VisaLong-term study visa (D) → Stamp 2 immigration permission
Funds to show€10,000 immediately available per year (€833 a month for 6–8-month courses); 6 months of bank statements; credit cards not accepted; pay at least €6,000 of fees (or all fees if lower) before applying
Work while studying20 hours a week in term; 40 hours in holidays (June–September and 15 Dec–15 Jan)
After graduationStamp 1G (Third Level Graduate Programme): full-time work, no business; length depends on degree level (up to 12 months at level 8)
